Project at a glance

A brand system people can use

Identity, website, internal asset library and presentation templates show how a visual language becomes a practical system across teams.

The brief

Modernize an established brand, then make it easy to follow.

Working Solutions pioneered the remote contact-center model. Fourteen years in, the identity had splintered into sub-brands and one-off pieces. The rebrand had two jobs: look like the company it had become, and give a company with no brand standards a reason to adopt them.

One scalable identity

Websites, internal apps, presentations, signage, sales collateral, documents and every sub-brand needed to draw from the same system.

Change behavior, build adoption

Guidelines alone don’t change habits. People needed tools that made the right choice the easy one: templates, a deck master and a single place to find assets.

My contribution

Translate the rebrand strategy into the visual identity and carry it through digital, print, presentation and environmental applications.

The audit

A brand built for a different era.

The original identity was about working “out of the box.” Over time, each team had made its own version. Laying every piece side by side made the case for change better than any slide could. Select a piece to enlarge it.

No shared system

Four sub-brands used four different marks, palettes and type choices.

Dated cues

Glossy 3D renders, stock world maps and road signs talked about technology, not people.

The wrong story

“Out of the box” described where agents work, not the value clients buy.

Strategy into form

From where we work to what we do.

Research pointed to one insight: the company’s value wasn’t the remote model, it was communication. Each of five directions was tested in one-color, CMYK, PMS and reverse, down to one inch, so the winner would hold up on a business card and a building wall.

Direction 01

Network

Fused dots stand for a nationwide network of agents connected by technology instead of a brick-and-mortar call center.

Why it lost: the dot field filled in below an inch and read as “tech company,” not “people.”

Direction 02

Network, refined

A denser dot field with an italic wordmark, tested in one-color, CMYK, PMS and reverse to see how the dots held up.

Why it lost: better at small sizes, but still the same story as the first direction.

Direction 03

Partnering

A W and an S merge into one mark. The letter ends rise like a growth chart: partnering with Working Solutions pays off over time.

Why it lost: a strong monogram, but it needed explaining.

Direction 04

Wordmark

A stripped-back, type-only direction in a friendly sans serif. Clean and flexible.

Why it lost: on its own it said nothing about what the company does.

Direction 05, approved

Conversation

The O becomes a speech bubble and the R gains a forward arrow. The idea lives inside the name itself, with nothing to bolt on.

Why it won: it told the new story, held up at every size, and gave the system a graphic device to build with.

Launching the brand online

From brochure site to a two-path experience.

The new site had to serve both audiences the business depends on: companies buying services, and people looking for work-from-home jobs. Hover the new homepage to scroll it.

  • Two clear paths. “My company needs your services” and “I want to work from home” sit at the top and repeat lower down.
