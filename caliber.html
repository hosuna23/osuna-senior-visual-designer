Caliber

Functioning as art director across three lines of business

I built the creative-review process, the brand color system and the campaign work for Caliber’s Auto Care, Collision and Auto Glass teams, as a Creative Circle contractor working without the art director title.

Context

Building a process where none existed.

Art requests arrived ad hoc, with no consistent review before work went live. I set up a working process and became the single point of contact for each line of business’s senior marketing director, so every piece had a clear path from request to approval.

1

Request

Directors submit needs by line of business, with channel, size and go-live date up front.

2

Creative direction

I set the concept, pick photography and choose the line-of-business treatment.

3

Brand review

A short checklist: color, logo lockup, type hierarchy, disclaimers and alt text.

4

Approval

Sign-off from the right senior marketing director before anything ships.

Fewer revision rounds

Briefs that arrive complete mean the first draft answers the right question.

One voice per brand

A single reviewer kept tone and color consistent across all three businesses.

My contribution

Art direction, campaign assets and brand documentation, owning creative decisions for three senior marketing directors.

Brand system

A color for each line of business.

All three businesses share one master brand. Giving each its own color makes them recognizable in a busy feed, without breaking the master identity. Switch between them to see the treatment.

Auto Care, orange

Warm and friendly for maintenance: seasonal tips, community days and quick polls that invite comments.

Auto Glass, light blue

Clear and calm for safety messages and recruiting, with plenty of room for one strong headline.

Collision, green

Used for the Oregon Legacy Project: hiring, team spotlights and teammate benefits.

Corporate campaign

Leaders telling their own stories.

A campaign spotlighting Caliber’s leadership team, mostly women, in their own words. I chose the brand’s pink to give the series its own look, then held the same palette, quote treatment and photography style across social, a landing page and an email.

Scroll the quote cards sideways, select one to enlarge

Email

Rules that held the series together

  • One pink from the master palette, never a new color
  • Quote first, name and title second, photo always right
  • A rainbow rule under every headline as the campaign signature
Internal communications

Guidelines for two technician-training programs.

With the VP of Marketing, I documented how Collision, Auto Glass and ProTech should appear alongside the TAP and Changing Lanes program logos, from polos and certificates to roll-up banners. Browse the full 24-page guide.

TAP

For people with little or no experience, working toward technician certification in Collision, Auto Glass or ProTech.

Changing Lanes

For military veterans and reservists moving into a technical career across the same three businesses.

Recognition at a glance

Know the brand before you read it.

Giving each line of business its own color meant people could tell Auto Care from Collision in a busy feed without reading a word. Big-screen browsing works the same way: art direction and color have to identify content from across the room.

Color as wayfinding

Orange, green and light blue separate three businesses inside one master brand.

Consistent art direction

One photo style and quote treatment across social, landing page and email.

Results

Landing experiences increased engagement by 45% and customer interaction by 20%.

Process at scale

A request, review and approval path kept quality high across three teams.
